## Evidence review

Each assigned editor directly read the local English source supporting their items. The MCQ editor read all 17 non-reference segments, examined the retained Figure 1 and Table 3 images, and checked the final short-answer questions against their assigned evidence. Original PDFs are absent; this review does not independently establish fidelity to the entire original publication or recheck the primary research cited by the article.

- `PRIOR-001`, `METHOD-SAMPLE-001`: CLOC is a prospective observational study. Its preloss observations and continuously married comparison group help distinguish widowhood-related patterns from aging, time, and some selection differences. They do not randomly assign bereavement or remove every confounder. The source's stronger wording about eliminating selection bias was not adopted as an unrestricted claim.
- `METHOD-SAMPLE-001`: the original sampling frame comprised noninstitutionalized English-speaking married couples in metropolitan Detroit with husbands aged at least 65. This is not an eligibility rule requiring every wife to be at least 65. The baseline sample was 1,532 individuals, with a 68% response rate. Women were oversampled to obtain more bereaved respondents. Controls were matched on age, race, and sex.
- `METHOD-SAMPLE-001`: the weighted analytic total is 297, comprising 210 widowed respondents and 87 controls. The actual unweighted sample is 333, comprising 249 widowed respondents and 84 controls. These are not competing counts for the same weighting convention. Primary Wave 1 interviews occurred six months after the spouse's death, not six months after baseline. The methods describe an 18- and 48-month follow-up schedule; only the six-month models are the main analyses here, with similar 18-month coefficients reported separately in the text but not displayed.
- `METHOD-MEASURES-001`: formal participation includes volunteering, meeting attendance, and religious services; informal participation includes visits and telephone contact with friends or relatives. The two outcome scales were standardized and higher scores mean more participation. Their reported internal consistency differs. The source asks about volunteer hours and later describes all items with daily-to-monthly frequency categories, an ambiguous scale description. The learning materials avoid imposing that identical frequency range on every item.
- `METHOD-CONFOUNDERS-001`: most covariates refer to baseline; depression is measured at Wave 1. Respondent health, spousal health and caregiving, extraversion, employment, driving, children, and socioeconomic circumstances are distinct variables. Associations do not establish that any one characteristic alone causes participation.
- `METHOD-ANALYSIS-001`, `DISCUSSION-THEORY-001`: the initial analysis maps positive and negative widowhood coefficients to activity and disengagement hypotheses, respectively, and positive baseline participation coefficients to continuity. A coefficient direction alone does not verify the mechanism of an entire theory. In particular, additional support initiated by others would not necessarily demonstrate active replacement of lost roles.
- `RESULTS-DESCRIPTIVE-001`: the retained Figure 1 agrees with the text that controls declined in both participation domains while widowed respondents had relatively stable unadjusted means. Future widowed respondents had lower baseline informal participation, and poor or fair spousal health was associated with lower preloss informal participation. Depression differences refer to Wave 1, while spousal caregiving and health differences refer to baseline.
- `RESULTS-MODELS-001`: the .44 value is the adjusted widowed-versus-control difference in standardized informal participation in Model 3, conditional on baseline participation and covariates. It is neither a 44% rise in contact frequency nor a uniform individual pre/post change. Formal widowhood coefficients were nonsignificant. Baseline participation coefficients were .58 formally and .31 informally; adjusted R² rose from .13 to .46 and from .16 to .29. The MCQ asks about the contribution of baseline participation rather than memorizing four fit statistics.
- `RESULTS-MODELS-001`: MCQ personality/depression directions are explicitly limited to Model 2. The retained Table 3 supports the positive .09/.11 extraversion coefficients and negative informal depression coefficient. However, the prose labels formal extraversion .09 as p ≤ .05, while the table shows .09 with superscript `a`, identifying a widowhood interaction, and no conventional significance star. This unresolved text/table marking difference is disclosed in the MCQ explanation; the question asks about coefficient directions, not the disputed significance claim.
- `RESULTS-MODELS-001`: the nonsignificant Widowhood × Sex interaction is not proof of identical effects for all men and women. Reported variation by income, education, race, and childlessness is interpreted in relation to unequal resources and circumstances. Childlessness in the subgroup discussion is not conflated with the general main-effect coefficient or made an inherent individual deficit.
- `RESULTS-MOTIVES-001`: reports of others' increased interest in contact (35%) differ from personal increased interest (17%) and unchanged personal interest (71%). These are the bereaved respondents' perceptions, not separate interviews with every friend or relative. They suggest that others may rally around survivors but do not establish a sole causal mechanism. The 87% reporting keeping busy to cope is not an 87% measured increase in participation.
- `DISCUSSION-SUMMARY-001`: widowhood can be a process beginning during spousal illness and continuing after death. Linked lives and differing psychological, familial, and socioeconomic resources provide interpretive context. This does not require that every survivor follow the same trajectory.
- `DISCUSSION-THEORY-001`: observed stability between waves cannot establish continuity throughout life or rule out earlier network selection. The discussion's claim that all participants were older than 65 is broader than the explicit husband-age eligibility criterion in the methods. MCQ 12 removes the blanket age claim and focuses on unobserved earlier-life histories. Socioemotional selectivity and social convoy models are distinguished, and proposed developmental explanations are not presented as comprehensively validated alternatives.
- `DISCUSSION-LIMITS-001`, `DISCUSSION-PRACTICE-001`: frequency does not measure subjective meaning or emotional quality. Suggested research considers perceived stress and both spouses' health and arrangements. Proposed support emphasizes continuity of meaningful relationships and roles, transport and communication, resource constraints, and support during preloss illness. These are recommendations from observational findings, not proven comparative intervention effects or a requirement to maximize activity for its own sake.

The short-answer owner replaced several numerical questions with prospective design, matching characteristics, outcome standardization, sources of contact, baseline participation, and extraversion. Only the .44-SD adjusted difference and six-month primary interview timing remain numerical answers. All final questions and explanations were checked against the assigned source segments; spellings and concise aliases were retained without exposing accepted answers in the prompts.
